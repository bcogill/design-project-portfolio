#About Me

My name is Brandon Cogill, I am in my third year of my mechanical engineering major here at UNC Charlotte. I have experience working at NGK Ceramics working the shaping and grinding department, learning and working with their engineers on future designs for their mass produced catalytic converters. I am hoping to get a internship next summer, where I can expand my knowledge even further and hopefully be a contribution to a company of professional engineers. 

The idea of creativity and building brought me closer and closer to the idea of wanting to major in Mechanical Engineering. I am an engineer that tries to find the best possible way to incorporate all forms of math, physics, and science to achieve a certain goal. Even when that goal of mine might be to far in the future I am focused on building and learning towards it everyday till I achieve it. I've always been fascinated with rockets and technology that I thought were never possible until I had seen them with my own eyes. As I've worked with other engineers throughout school I have learn that everyone has there own personal interests and I think without their input and advice I wouldn't be the engineer that I set myself to be. The one thing that I think brought me to Mechanical Engineering was those first basic classes that you take in high school, where you are just dipping your toes in and were given more freedom to create what you wanted. I joined a program called NAF that guided me through all different areas of engineering, showing me all the engineering concepts such as Civil, Electrical, Environmental, and yes Mechanical. I think I am becoming a engineer that likes to be hit with new ideas and thoughts, making my mind think a little on why those ideas could have a huge or little impact on something that I do in the near future. I want to be that engineer that everyone just finds as a harder working, chill, friend. I want to be able to solve problems that make me feel like I went home everyday saying "I did something great today" and no matter what that is or if anyone see's it, I hope it made a impact. 

**"What does it mean to defend an engineering decision : and do you currently know how to do it?"**

Defending a engineering decision is like holding standing ground and sticking with the original plan, not letting anyone change what you think whether its your calculations or the design of a entire project. As a engineer you should have hopefully thought of every type of response to your decision whether its criticism or advice. I don't think that I currently know how to do it at this moment in time but I know that in the future when I and many of engineers around the world will have done enough research and testing to fully defend and engineering decision that is thrown our way.
